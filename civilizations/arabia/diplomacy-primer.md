# Diplomatic Mechanics Primer

*How diplomacy works in Civ 6, and how Arabia uses it.*

*[City-States →](city-states-reference.md) | [Situations →](situations.md)*

---

## The Diplomatic Relationship System

Every civ has a relationship level with every other civ. It ranges from:
```
Denounced → Unfriendly → Neutral → Friendly → Allied
```

Relationship level affects:
- Whether they'll trade with you
- Whether they'll grant Open Borders
- How likely they are to declare war
- World Congress voting alignment

**Arabia's diplomatic goal:** Stay neutral-to-friendly with most civs. Strong relations = Open Borders for religion spreading.

---

## Basic Diplomatic Actions

### Sending a Delegation
- **Cost:** 25 Gold
- **Effect:** +3 to relationship score with that civ
- **When:** Send a Delegation to every civ you meet within 1–2 turns of meeting them
- **Why:** Prevents the "Harboring Grudges" penalty, keeps early relationship stable

### Declaring Friendship
- **Effect:** +3 relationship score; both civs commit to not attacking each other
- **Risk:** If you later attack them, you take a −30 "Betrayed a Declaration of Friendship" penalty with all civs
- **When:** Use with civs who share your religion or are clearly non-threatening

### Alliances
- **Available from:** Classical era (Political Philosophy civic)
- **Types:** Research Alliance, Economic Alliance, Military Alliance, Religious Alliance
- **Effect:** Both civs gain benefits over time; the longer the alliance, the stronger it grows
- **For Arabia:**
  - **Religious Alliance** — both civs' religions spread faster; useful if ally follows your religion
  - **Research Alliance** — both gain Science from the alliance; good for Science Victory
  - **Economic Alliance** — both gain bonus Trade Route yields; combines with Triangular Trade

## War Types

Not all wars are equal. The type of war you declare determines diplomatic penalties.

| War Type | Diplomatic Penalty | Requirements | Arabia Use Case |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|----------------|
| **Surprise War** | −40 "Warmonger" penalty | None — just declare | Almost never worth it |
| **Formal War** | −20 penalty | Declare at least 5 turns after meeting | Standard expansion wars |
| **Holy War** | **0 penalty** | Target must have a different religion in their cities | **Arabia's primary war type** |
| **Liberation War** | **0 penalty** | Liberate a city previously taken from another civ | Situational |
| **Reconquest War** | 0 penalty | Reclaim your own previously captured city | Rare but useful |
| **Colonial War** | Reduced penalty | Target is on a different continent | Archipelago maps |

> **Holy War is Arabia's diplomatic weapon.** If a rival civ has a different religion in their cities, you can declare Holy War at zero cost. This lets you fight without the "Warmonger" reputation that makes everyone hate you. This is how Arabia goes to war cleanly.

## Warmonger Penalties

The "Warmonger" penalty applies when you capture or raze cities. It makes all other civs distrust you.

**How it works:**
- Each city you capture: −X Warmonger penalty with all civs
- The penalty decays slowly over turns
- Razing a city is worse than keeping it
- Surprise Wars add a flat penalty on top

**How Arabia avoids it:**
1. Declare Holy Wars instead of Surprise/Formal Wars
2. Keep captured cities rather than razing them (razing is much worse)
3. Capture only capitals when going for Domination (each capital has a fixed penalty; secondary cities are cheaper)
4. Use Peace Deals to end wars before more cities change hands

## Open Borders

Open Borders agreements allow your units to move through a civ's territory freely.

**Why Arabia needs them:**
- Your Missionaries and Apostles can only spread religion to cities they can reach
- Without Open Borders, your units stop at the border — you can't spread to distant cities
- Open Borders to a civ = passive religious pressure via movement

**How to get them:**
- **Trade screen:** Negotiate Open Borders (offer Gold/turn or a resource in exchange)
- **Alliance:** All alliance types include mutual Open Borders
- **High relationship score:** Friendly+ civs usually grant Open Borders for free/cheap

**For Religious Victory:** By Turn 120–140, try to have Open Borders with every civ you need to convert. This is often the difference between reaching 51% and stalling.

## World Congress

The World Congress becomes available after the Medieval era (Renaissance typically). All civs vote on resolutions that affect the entire game.

### How It Works
- Each civ gets votes proportional to their Diplomatic Favor
- **Diplomatic Favor** comes from: being Suzerain of city-states, Alliance bonuses, Wonders, some policy cards
- Resolutions pass if more votes are for than against
- New session every 30 turns or so; 2–3 resolutions per session

### Key Resolutions Arabia Cares About

| Resolution | Effect | Arabia Vote |
|-----------|--------|------------|
| **Defender of the Faith** | All units of the majority religion get +5 CS | FOR (if your religion is majority) |
| **Religious Patronage** | Extra Envoys to religious city-states | FOR |
| **Religious Rites** | Holy Site buildings produce more Faith | FOR |
| **Economic Policy** | +2 Gold per Trade Route | FOR |
| **Heritage Tourism** | Tourism bonuses | AGAINST (helps Cultural Victory opponents) |
| **Emergency sanctions** against a civ | Collective bonuses for attacking that civ | SITUATIONAL |

> **Diplomatic Favor strategy for Arabia:** Become Suzerain of as many city-states as possible (Envoys), maintain Alliances for bonus Favor, and vote strategically. Your religion-following allies will vote with you if the resolution benefits religion.

## Grievances and Diplomatic Modifiers

Certain actions create lasting relationship damage ("Grievances"):
- Settling too close to their cities: −10 to −20
- Violating their borders without Open Borders: −10
- Pillaging their improvements: −20
- Betraying a Declaration of Friendship: −30

**Things Arabia should avoid:**
- Settling within 3 tiles of an AI civ's border without intending to fight (it's provocative and not worth it)
- Pillaging Trade Routes (creates Grievances with all civs)
- Declaring Surprise War (large, lasting penalty)

---

## Delegation vs. Declaration of Friendship vs. Alliance

| Action | When | Cost | Risk |
|--------|------|------|------|
| **Delegation** | On meeting every civ | 25 Gold once | None |
| **Declaration of Friendship** | T50+ with non-threatening civs | None | Breaking it is costly |
| **Alliance** | T100+ with aligned civs | Commitment period | Locked into supporting them |

**Rule:** Send a Delegation to everyone. Declare Friendship with religion-sharing civs. Alliance only with civs you're confident won't become enemies.

## Religion and Diplomacy

Arabia's religious strategy is deeply tied to diplomacy:

- **Ayyubid Dynasty (Vizier's agenda):** Saladin likes civs that follow his religion, dislikes those that don't. As Arabia, this means: spreading your religion to AI civs improves their attitude toward you.
- **Religious Alliance:** If you share a religion with an ally, the Religious Alliance type boosts both your spreads.
- **Holy War:** Covered above — this is your war-without-penalty mechanism.
- **Theological Combat:** Apostles and Missionaries can fight other religious units. This is "warfare" within the religious domain — it doesn't trigger political war, but repeated theological aggression can sour relations.

## Emergency Declarations

Occasionally the World Congress calls an Emergency — all civs can vote to collectively punish a civ that has done something outrageous (nuclear bomb, captured a city belonging to a player at peace, etc.).

**Arabia in Emergencies:**
- If an aggressive military civ is dominating, voting FOR the Emergency collectively weakens them — do it
- If you're the target (you took someone's city), everyone getting bonuses against you is painful — try to avoid the trigger conditions

---

## Diplomatic Victory (Reference)

Arabia doesn't primarily pursue Diplomatic Victory, but it's available as a fallback.

**How it works:**
- The World Congress awards Diplomatic Victory Points for winning certain resolutions
- Reaching a threshold of DVP wins the game
- Arabia's Tithe Gold income can be used to purchase Diplomatic Favor (and thus votes)

**When to consider it:** If Religious Victory is stalling at 45–48% and Science is too far away, and you have significant accumulated Gold — buying Diplomatic Favor and winning World Congress votes is a third path.
